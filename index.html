Is It a Water Heater Emergency? How to Tell and What to Do Tonight

Not every water heater problem is an emergency, and some that seem minor are. Sorting them quickly tells you whether to call 911, shut things off and wait, or simply book a visit.

Use this page to sort the situation in front of you. For problems that can wait until business hours, RapidHeat Stockton connects Stockton-area homeowners with local professionals for emergency water heater help during its phone hours, Monday to Friday 8AM to 6PM and Saturday 9AM to 2PM. It is a referral service, not an emergency responder. For anything in the first group below, call 911.

Call 911 Now

  • You smell gas. Leave on foot without touching switches or using a flame or flashlight. Call 911, then PG&E, from a safe distance.
  • A carbon monoxide alarm sounds, or people feel dizzy, headachy or sick indoors. Get everyone outside and call 911.
  • Fire, sparks or smoke at the heater or its wiring.
  • Someone is seriously burned.

Urgent: Shut Off and Contain Tonight

These need action now, but not emergency services. Shut things off, limit damage, and arrange service.

What is happeningWhat to do now
Water pouring or spreading from the heaterClose the cold supply valve above the heater. Turn the gas control to off or switch off the breaker. Keep away from wet outlets.
Relief valve releasing hot water or steamTurn off gas or power. Close the cold supply. Stay clear of the discharge pipe. Do not cap the valve.
Water at the taps is suddenly scaldingTurn off gas or power to the heater. Warn everyone in the house. The thermostat or control may have failed.
Rumbling that is getting violent, or the tank looks swollenTurn off gas or power and the cold supply. Do not use hot water.

Can Wait Until Morning

  • No hot water, nothing else wrong. Check the breaker or the pilot. If it will not come back, turn off gas or power and book a visit.
  • Lukewarm water or hot water running out early. Often sediment, an element, or sizing.
  • A slow drip from a fitting. Put a pan under it, check it is not getting worse, and book a visit.
  • Popping or crackling. Usually sediment. Not dangerous on its own.
  • Rusty or smelly hot water. Anode or tank condition. Book a visit.

The Three Shutoffs to Know

  1. Cold water supply valve, on the pipe going into the top of the heater.
  2. Gas shutoff, on the gas line to the heater. The handle is open when parallel to the pipe and closed when crosswise.
  3. Breaker, for an electric heater, usually a double breaker in the main panel.

Find and label all three before you ever need them.

Getting Through the Night Without Hot Water

  • Heat water on the stove or in a kettle for washing.
  • Run the dishwasher, which heats its own water on many models.
  • Keep the heater off until it has been looked at if there was a leak or scalding water.

What to Tell the Professional

Have the brand, model and age of the heater, what happened, what you shut off, and photos if there was a leak. That shortens the diagnosis and helps with any insurance claim.

For safety basics, the U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission at cpsc.gov recommends a 120°F setting to reduce scald risk once the heater is back in service.
